# EDA Report — Clinical Trial Insights and Patterns

**Dataset:** `clinical_trials_raw_patient2trial_conditions.csv` (ClinicalTrials.gov records retrieved for 8 conditions)
**After cleaning:** 60,024 trials × 8 disease categories · **NLP input:** `brief_summary` · **Label:** `source_condition_query` → `disease_category`

All figures are in [`figures/`](figures/) and all tables in [`eda_tables/`](eda_tables/). They are regenerated by `python run_pipeline.py`.

---

## 1. Data understanding & cleaning

| Item | Value |
|---|---|
| Raw shape | 60,337 rows × 16 columns |
| Missing cells (raw) | 80,526 — `phase` 37,001 · `maximum_age` 31,937 · `interventions` 6,061 · `minimum_age` 3,216 · `healthy_volunteers` 1,480 · `official_title` 786 · `sex` 33 · `eligibility_criteria` 11 · `conditions` 1 |
| Missing `brief_summary` / label | 0 / 0 |
| Exact duplicate rows / duplicate `nct_id` | 0 / 0 |
| Identical summaries with **conflicting** labels (dropped, ambiguous) | 30 rows |
| Repeated summaries with the same label (one copy kept) | 262 rows |
| Summaries with < 3 meaningful tokens after cleaning (dropped) | 21 rows |
| **Final rows** | **60,024** (99.5% of raw) |

Missing-value handling: `phase` missing = *Not Applicable* (observational, device and behavioural studies have no drug phase); `official_title` ← `title`; `interventions` / `eligibility_criteria` ← *Not specified*; `sex` ← mode (*ALL*); `healthy_volunteers` ← *Unknown*. Ages were converted from text (`"18 Years"`, `"6 Months"`) to numeric years. A missing minimum age means no lower limit (0). A missing maximum age means **no upper limit**, so it is kept as a flag instead of being filled with an invented number. After cleaning there are 0 missing cells apart from that intentional flag.

Removing duplicate summaries also means the **same text can't appear in both the training and test sets**, which would otherwise inflate test accuracy.

## 3. Summary text statistics — `eda_02_summary_length.png`

| | mean | median | max |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Words per summary (raw) | 102 | 69 | 799 |
| Tokens after cleaning + lemmatization | 50 | 34 | 428 |
| Lexical diversity (unique / total tokens) | 0.81 | 0.82 | 1.00 |

* Summary length is strongly right-skewed: most summaries are short (25% have ≤ 37 words), with a long tail of detailed protocols.
* **Anxiety** (median 93 words), **Sickle Cell Anemia** (83) and **COVID-19** (77) have the longest summaries. **Glaucoma** (45) and **Rheumatoid Arthritis** (53) have the shortest, mostly concise drug and IOP studies.
* Cleaning removes about **half of all words** (stop words, punctuation, numbers and clinical boiler-plate such as *study, patient, trial*).
* Correlations (`eda_10_feature_correlation.png`): longer summaries have lower lexical diversity (r = −0.66) and mention more disease categories (r = 0.23). Trial metadata (number of interventions, minimum age) is unrelated to text length.

## 4. Most common medical conditions and keywords — `eda_03`, `eda_04`

**Most frequent terms** (after cleaning): `treatment` (in 29.8% of summaries), `cancer`, `breast`, `disease`, `control`, `safety`, `therapy`, `efficacy`, `covid-19`, `diabetes`.
**Top bigrams:** *breast cancer*, *type diabetes* (from "type 2 diabetes"), *quality life*, *efficacy safety*, *diabetes mellitus*, *rheumatoid arthritis*, *mental health*, *chronic obstructive*, *physical activity*, *safety tolerability*.

Generic words such as *treatment*, *control* and *efficacy* appear in every category. TF-IDF down-weights them. The **distinctive terms** (chi² over TF-IDF, one category vs the rest) show the vocabulary of each disease:

| Category | Most distinctive terms |
|---|---|
| Breast Cancer | breast, cancer, tumor, chemotherapy, metastatic, her2, neoadjuvant, woman |
| Type 2 Diabetes | diabetes, insulin, mellitus, glucose, t2dm, metformin, glycemic control |
| COVID-19 | covid-19, infection, pandemic, coronavirus, vaccine, virus, acute respiratory |
| Anxiety | anxiety, disorder, depression, mental, virtual reality, child, ocd, pain anxiety |
| COPD | copd, pulmonary, obstructive, exacerbation, lung, airway, asthma |
| Rheumatoid Arthritis | arthritis, rheumatoid, ra, methotrexate, disease activity, mtx, sjogren, adalimumab |
| Glaucoma | glaucoma, ocular, intraocular pressure, iop, eye, open-angle, ocular hypertension |
| Sickle Cell Anemia | sickle cell, scd, hydroxyurea, vaso-occlusive crisis, anemia, child |

Beyond the disease names, the summaries carry **treatment and biomarker signals**: metformin and insulin (diabetes), methotrexate and adalimumab (RA), hydroxyurea (sickle cell), HER2 and neoadjuvant chemotherapy (breast cancer), IOP (glaucoma), vaccines (COVID-19), and virtual reality as an anxiety intervention.

## 5. Relationship between summaries and categories — `eda_09_disease_mentions.png`

| Category | Names its own disease | Mentions 2+ disease categories |
|---|---:|---:|
| Breast Cancer | 90.1% | 5.8% |
| Type 2 Diabetes | 89.0% | 8.2% |
| COVID-19 | 91.1% | 17.6% |
| Anxiety | **67.9%** | 6.9% |
| COPD | 76.7% | 5.6% |
| Rheumatoid Arthritis | 72.7% | 8.0% |
| Glaucoma | 87.1% | 3.1% |
| Sickle Cell Anemia | 86.5% | 11.4% |

* Most summaries name their own disease, but **about 1 in 3 Anxiety summaries do not**. Anxiety is often an *outcome* measured in surgery, dental, cancer or ICU studies, so these trials use the vocabulary of other conditions. As the model evaluation confirms, Anxiety is the class other trials are most often misclassified *into* (it has the lowest precision).
* **COVID-19** summaries most often mention a second disease category (17.6%). These are COVID-19 in people with diabetes, COPD or cancer, or anxiety during the pandemic.
* Some trials therefore belong to **more than one category** in reality. The single label comes from the search query, so some label noise is unavoidable.

## 6. Clinical-trial patterns and trends

### Phase mix — `eda_05_phase_by_category.png`
* **Anxiety** trials are 86% *Not Applicable* phase (behavioural and psychological interventions, not drugs). **COVID-19** is 67% (many observational studies).
* **Breast Cancer** (21% Phase 2) and **Sickle Cell Anemia** (18% Phase 2, 7% Phase 1/2) are the most early-development and drug-pipeline heavy.
* **Glaucoma** (13% Phase 4), **Type 2 Diabetes** (12%) and **RA** (10%) have the most Phase 4 (post-marketing) studies, which fits mature treatment markets.

### Status — `eda_06_status_by_category.png`, `eda_07_design_population.png`
* Highest completion rate: **Type 2 Diabetes 65.9%**. Lowest: **Breast Cancer 45.9%**, where 15.1% are still recruiting because oncology trials run long.
* Highest terminated, withdrawn or suspended share: **Sickle Cell Anemia 14.9%** and Breast Cancer 12.1%. Lowest: Anxiety 5.3%.
* **COVID-19** has the largest *Unknown* status share (26.3%). Many pandemic-era registrations were never updated.

### Population & design — `eda_07`, `eda_08`
* **Breast Cancer**: 62.4% of trials enrol **women only**. Every other category is ≤ 11%.
* **Sickle Cell Anemia** is the **paediatric** category: 64.6% of trials accept children (median minimum age 12 years). The overall rate is 13.0%.
* **COVID-19** is the least interventional category (57.8%, many registries and cohorts). **Anxiety** is the most interventional (86.4%).
* Healthy volunteers are accepted most often in **Anxiety** (37.2%) and **COVID-19** (33.5%) trials, for example stress reduction and vaccine studies.

## 7. Key takeaways
1. The text is highly informative. Each disease has a clear vocabulary of disease names, drugs, biomarkers and procedures, so TF-IDF with a linear model should work well.
2. **Anxiety** is a cross-cutting symptom. A third of its summaries never name it, and it overlaps with cancer, surgery and COVID-19 studies, so it is the main source of confusion between categories.
3. The class imbalance (14 : 1) calls for stratified splitting and macro-averaged metrics, with balanced class weights considered during tuning.
4. Trial-design patterns differ a lot by disease (female-only breast cancer trials, paediatric sickle-cell trials, behavioural anxiety trials, observational COVID-19 studies). These are useful for research portfolio planning.
